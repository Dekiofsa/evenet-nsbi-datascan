# Training-statistics scan: tier 2 results (seed repeats and the frozen-backbone control)

Ailun Shen, 13 September 2026. Follows `results_tier1.md`; the tier-1 diagnostics still hold and are extended here to all seeds.

## Runs

Tier 2 added 34 trainings on the L4: seeds 1 and 2 for both the scratch and the fully fine-tuned pretrained model at 1, 3 and 10 % (24 runs, both CARL classifiers each), and the frozen-backbone control (pretrained EveNet with all transformer weights frozen, only the 1.19M-parameter head trained) at all five fractions with seed 0 (10 runs). Everything else is as in tier 1: the same optimizer-step budget (2,000 steps per "epoch", up to 50 epochs, early stopping with patience 5 on a fixed validation subset), the same validation split, the same 48 pseudo-experiments per mu value at 300 fb^-1 and the same 1M-event calibration reference. Seed k changes the training subsample (subsample seed 1000 + k), the network initialisation of the scratch model and the sampling order. The 56 runs in the tree also include the two poster-checkpoint pseudo-runs (`runs/poster/`, exported from the May checkpoints for the pipeline check of tier 1).

The 34 runs took 35.7 run-hours on the L4 (0.35 to 2.2 h each, two at a time on the shared GPU), 22 h of wall-clock from launch to the last run. Tier 1 took 29.6 run-hours. The measurement and all diagnostics below run on a laptop CPU.

## Findings

1. The seed repeats settle the tier-1 question: the scratch curve is reproducible, the pretrained failure is systematic. At 3 and 10 % the scratch bias at mu = 2.5 spreads by 0.04 and 0.07 over seeds (toy error 0.01, statistical half-width 0.28), and at mu = 0.4 all six points lie within 0.12 of zero. The fully fine-tuned pretrained model fails in eight of the nine seed-fraction points at 1 to 10 %: at mu = 2.5 eight of nine have open intervals or sit at mu_hat = 0, and at mu = 0.4 seven of nine spike near mu = 1 or collapse; the one point that fails at neither, 3 % seed 2, gives -0.36 with closed intervals and a partial spike (+0.25, 20 of 48 toys near mu = 1), still worse than scratch. It recovers only at 30 and 100 %, where it matches scratch at mu = 2.5 (-0.22 vs -0.27, -0.17 vs -0.18) but still spikes at 30 % for mu = 0.4. There is no training size at which pretraining improves the measurement; the paired per-seed difference of absolute biases favours the pretrained model only at 3 % seed 2 (+0.11), 30 % (+0.05) and 100 % (+0.004 and +0.06), all inside the seed spread and well inside the statistical half-width of 0.24 to 0.40.

2. At 1 % the scratch model is itself at the edge. Its three seeds give -1.29 (17 open), -0.74 and -0.74 at mu = 2.5, and at mu = 0.4 seed 1 shows the same mu = 1 spike as the pretrained model (+0.56, interval width 0.21): that seed's S/B ratio has the heaviest tail of any scratch run (validation background events with r > 30 carry 20 % of the calibration average <r>_B, against 3 and 6 % for the other two seeds). The tail pathology is therefore a low-statistics phenomenon of the CARL ratio that full fine-tuning of the pretrained network makes much worse, not something specific to pretraining.

3. Pretraining does help the classifier, but only at 1 %, and there the better classifier gives the worse measurement. On the full validation set the mean over three seeds of the S/B cross-entropy is 0.460 (fine-tuned) against 0.543 (scratch) at 1 %, with AUC 0.864 against 0.852. At 3 % the order is already reversed (0.447 against 0.436) and stays reversed to 100 % (0.430 against 0.427, one seed each; the poster's May checkpoint, trained longer with a different schedule, has 0.425). The frozen head is the weakest classifier at every fraction above 1 % (0.453 to 0.444). So the regime where transfer wins on the classifier metric is exactly the regime where its density-ratio tails break the likelihood.

4. Why the fine-tuned model breaks: it fits the small training set inside the first 2,000 steps. At 1 % every pretrained S/B run has its best validation loss after epoch 1 and the loss then climbs steeply (0.447, 0.491, 0.550, 0.669, 0.726, 0.802 for seed 0, while the training loss falls from 0.44 to 0.19); at 3 % the best epoch is 1 or 2 with the same picture. The kept model is the epoch-1 checkpoint, which is already past the optimum at a granularity of 2,000 optimizer steps, with all 19.9M parameters trainable at learning rates 1e-4 (head), 5e-5 (object encoder) and 1e-5 (transformer body). The scratch model at 1 % overfits even harder (training loss 0.39 to 0.04, validation 0.51 to 1.19) but its epoch-1 network, 2,000 steps from random initialisation, has not yet built the sharp features that produce ratios of 1,000; its tails are typically an order of magnitude lighter (maximum calibrated ratio on the toy events 57, 179 and 58 for the three scratch seeds at 1 % against 1,226, 594 and 173 for the fine-tuned ones, and up to 1,914 at 3 %). The frozen head overfits more slowly (validation loss 0.443 to 0.512 over six epochs at 1 %); its tails are intermediate at 1 to 10 % but exceed the fine-tuned ones at 30 and 100 %. Ratio tails across all runs, as the share of <r>_B on the validation background carried by events with r > 30: fine-tuned pretrained 14, 24, 21 % (1 %), 28, 22, 3 % (3 %), 14, 8, 18 % (10 %), 6 % (30 %), 0.6 % (100 %); scratch 3, 20, 6 % (1 %), 0, 4, 11 % (3 %), below 0.01 % at 10 and 100 % and 0.3 % at 30 %; frozen 10, 2.5, 4.7, 7.2, 7.5 % from 1 to 100 %. The same numbers can be read as an over-estimate: the share of <r>_B in the tail region is the classifier's own estimate of the signal probability mass there, and the signal validation sample actually puts far less weight there. The ratio of the two is 2.9 to 7.1 for the fine-tuned runs at 1 to 10 % (5.4 at 30 %, 4.0 at 100 %), 2.1 to 3.0 for scratch at 1 to 3 %, and 2.3 to 3.0 for the frozen head at every fraction (`diagnostics/tail_stats_per_run.csv`, column tail_overestimate). The tail share is a necessary but not sufficient predictor of failure: scratch 3 % seed 2 carries 11 % in the tail and still fits well.

5. Which classifier, with seeds and the frozen ladder. Pairing each S/B classifier with the SBI/B classifier of another model at the same fraction and seed (`diagnostics/cross_variant_pairs.csv`):
   - The pretrained S/B is the culprit in every seed. With the scratch SBI/B of the same seed it spikes near mu = 1 for mu = 0.4 in seven of the nine points at 1 to 10 % (35 to 48 of the 48 toys), partially in the other two (20 of 48) and at 30 % (39 toys); at mu = 2.5 it has open intervals or a bias of -0.70 or below in seven of the nine (the exceptions: 3 % seed 2 at -0.37 and 10 % seed 1 at -0.22). Only its 100 % version is healthy with any partner (-0.13 and -0.03).
   - The pretrained SBI/B is no better than the scratch one once seeds are included. With the scratch S/B of the same seed at 3 % it gives +0.02, -0.63 and -0.50 at mu = 2.5 for seeds 0, 1, 2 (scratch diagonal: -0.55, -0.48, -0.47), and at 10 % it collapses in seed 0 (the 1.4e6 outlier of tier 1), gives -0.69 with 9 open intervals in seed 1 and -0.25 in seed 2. The +0.02 quoted in tier 1 is the lucky end of the seed spread of a bias set by sub-percent calibration noise, not a property of the pretrained interference classifier.
   - The frozen offset lives entirely in its S/B head. The frozen S/B with the scratch SBI/B gives +0.38, +0.13, +0.45, +0.32, +0.35 at mu = 0.4 from 1 to 100 % (frozen diagonal: +0.41, +0.27, +0.41, +0.40, +0.36), while the scratch S/B with the frozen SBI/B gives +0.32, +0.03, -0.06, -0.01, -0.07, within 0.04 to 0.12 of the scratch diagonal and with no trace of the +0.3 to +0.4 offset. At mu = 2.5 the frozen SBI/B with the scratch S/B gives -0.16, -0.22, -0.14, -0.34 from 3 to 100 % (scratch diagonal -0.55, -0.20, -0.27, -0.18). The frozen interference classifier is therefore as good as any; the S/B head trained on frozen features is the weak piece.
   - Within each model, pairing every S/B with every SBI/B of the same seed (`diagnostics/attribution_per_seed.csv`) gives the same attribution. The mu = 0.4 result is set by the S/B row in every seed of every model: seven of the nine pretrained rows at 1 to 10 % read +0.50 to +0.59 whichever SBI/B they are paired with (except with the 10 % seed-0 SBI/B outlier), and the two rows that do not spike (3 % seed 2: +0.20 to +0.32; 10 % seed 1: +0.24 to +0.50) are the two non-spiking points of the headline table. At mu = 2.5 the scratch bias depends mostly on the SBI/B column in seeds 0 and 1 (a spread of 0.28 to 0.51 across columns within a row from 3 % on, against 0.05 to 0.19 across rows) and on both about equally in seed 2 (0.20 to 0.22 against 0.21 to 0.25), and the scratch 1 % S/B is marginal in all three seeds, with partly open intervals for some SBI/B partners (up to 29, 31 and 17 of 48 toys). For the frozen ladder the S/B row dominates at both mu values: the 3 % head is its best (-0.12 to -0.28 at mu = 2.5, +0.23 to +0.31 at mu = 0.4) and the 10 to 100 % heads are worse and flat (-0.28 to -0.44, +0.30 to +0.43; the 1 % head is the worst of all), in step with the tail share (2.5 % at 3 % against 4.7 to 7.5 % above).

6. The calibration sample decides the fine-tuned result, not the scratch one (all seeds). Recomputing every diagonal point with the calibration factor taken from the full validation background instead of the fixed 1M-event reference moves the scratch results at 3 % and above by at most 0.03 at mu = 2.5 and 0.06 at mu = 0.4 (one exception: 10 % seed 1, +0.14), and by up to 0.18 at 1 %. The fine-tuned pretrained results move by up to 1.7: at 10 % seeds 1 and 2 go from -1.21 and -0.74 to -2.23 and -2.46 (open intervals from 20 and 3 to 42 and 47 of 48) and the 30 % point from -0.22 to -0.58 (6 open). A result that depends on which background sample was used to normalise the ratio is the signature of a calibration average dominated by a handful of tail events. The frozen 1 % point improves from -1.60 (25 open) to -0.73 (4 open); the frozen ladder above 1 % moves by at most 0.03. The poster checkpoints move by -0.12 (mu = 2.5) and -0.17 (mu = 0.4), their two calibration factors differing by 3.2 %.

7. Capping the ratio tail and recalibrating removes the catastrophe in most seeds, but does not make pretraining win. Capping the calibrated S/B ratio at 30 (SBI/B at 3) and renormalising on the validation background (`diagnostics/clipping_per_seed.csv`, `figures/bias_clipped_vs_raw.png`):
   - Fine-tuned pretrained, mu = 2.5: 1 % -0.45, -1.88 (34 open), -0.53; 3 % -0.73 (9 open), -0.56 (2 open), -0.25; 10 % -0.38, -1.52 (27 open), -0.39; 30 % -0.13; 100 % -0.21. At mu = 0.4: 1 % +0.13, +0.42 (22 toys still at mu = 1), -0.17; 3 % +0.37 (33 at mu = 1), -0.10, -0.09; 10 % +0.04, -0.21, +0.17; 30 % +0.03; 100 % -0.08. At mu = 2.5 five of the nine low-statistics points become clean fits, two more keep a few open intervals (3 % seeds 0 and 1: 9 and 2 of 48) and two stay mostly open (1 % seed 1 with 34 open, and 10 % seed 1 with 27 open, worse than before capping); at mu = 0.4 all nine close their intervals and only 3 % seed 0 remains a majority spike (33 toys), with 1 % seed 1 partial (22). The two that stay broken at mu = 2.5 carry 24 % and only 8 % of <r>_B in the tail, while the heaviest tail of all (3 % seed 0, 28 %) is repaired to -0.73: a cap is not a complete fix, because the bulk of the ratio is miscalibrated as well.
   - Against scratch after the same treatment: at 1 % the two repaired pretrained seeds (-0.45, -0.53) do slightly better than scratch (-0.62, -0.74 and -1.22 with 15 open); at 3 % they are comparable (-0.56, -0.25 and the partly open -0.73 against -0.56, -0.48, -0.42); at 10 % they do worse (-0.38, -0.39 and one still mostly open against -0.21, -0.15, -0.28). Capping changes the scratch results by at most 0.06 at mu = 2.5 from 3 % on, and by up to 0.18 at mu = 0.4 (3 % seed 2, from the cap itself; 10 % seed 1 moves +0.14 from the recalibration alone). The tail treatment removes the failure; it does not open a regime in which the fine-tuned model measures mu better.
   - Clipping without recalibration is harmful: the seed-0 pretrained points stay at mu_hat = 0 with 48 open intervals at 1 and 10 % and sit at -2.48 at 3 %, and the frozen 1 % point worsens from -1.60 to -2.38, because removing the tail pulls <r>_B below 1 and the whole S/B ratio ends up scaled down. The renormalisation factor after capping is 1.01 to 1.28 for the fine-tuned runs at 1 to 30 %, 1.00 to 1.07 for scratch at 1 % and 1.01 to 1.05 for the frozen head; it is 1.00 for every SBI/B classifier except the 10 % seed-0 outlier (1.0065).
   - The scratch 1 % spike of seed 1 disappears as well (+0.56 to +0.02 at mu = 0.4), which confirms that it was the same tail mechanism.

8. The frozen backbone is the informative control, and much of its mu = 0.4 offset is a tail effect. From 3 % up it never collapses at mu = 2.5 (open intervals only at 1 %, where it gives -1.60 with 25 open) and never spikes fully at mu = 0.4, so the pretrained features transfer at low statistics and the failure of the fine-tuned model is a failure of the fine-tuning recipe. Its raw offset, +0.27 to +0.41 at mu = 0.4 and -0.19 to -0.42 at mu = 2.5 with no trend towards zero as the data grow, comes from its S/B head (finding 5). Capping that head's ratio at 30 and recalibrating reduces the mu = 0.4 offset to +0.33, +0.19, +0.11, +0.17, +0.02 from 1 to 100 % and the mu = 2.5 one to -0.48, -0.14, -0.26, -0.26, -0.36; at mu = 0.4 the cap removes 20 to 30 % of the offset at 1 and 3 % and 60 to 95 % from 10 % up; what remains at mu = 2.5 is close to the -0.15 to -0.29 that the scratch and fine-tuned models show from 10 % up. The head trained on frozen features is simply the weaker S/B classifier (validation cross-entropy 0.453 to 0.444 against 0.424 to 0.436 for scratch from 3 to 100 %), with a moderate tail of its own at every fraction.

## Conclusions

- With the poster fine-tuning recipe (all layers, learning rates 1e-4 to 1e-5, validation every 2,000 steps): nowhere. Below 30 % it breaks the measurement in eight of nine seed-fraction points and degrades the ninth; at 30 % it matches scratch at mu = 2.5 but still spikes at mu = 0.4; at 100 % it matches scratch at both. The frozen control shows the pretrained features themselves are usable from 3 % on, so the next test is the recipe, not the features: resume the kept 1 to 10 % pretrained checkpoints with a lower learning rate, validation every 250 to 500 steps and an annealing step, and see whether the epoch-1 optimum can be caught before the tails form.
- Independently of pretraining, the measurement needs a tail treatment: cap the calibrated S/B ratio (30 is a reasonable value) and recalibrate on a large background sample. That gives clean fits at mu = 2.5 in five of the nine fine-tuned low-statistics points and removes the mu = 1 spike in eight of nine at mu = 0.4, cures the scratch 1 % spike and much of the frozen offset at mu = 0.4 above 3 % (finding 7); clipping without recalibration makes things worse at mu = 2.5. It is not a complete fix (two fine-tuned points stay mostly open) and it does not turn the fine-tuned model into the better one.
- The residual -0.15 to -0.3 at mu = 2.5 in every healthy configuration from 10 % up, and the frozen offset, are the calibration-level effects of tier-1 finding 5 (half a percent on the SBI/B ratio decides the result). They are what an ensemble averages down and where the "bias term" of the ensemble study lives.

## Caveats

- The frozen ladder and the 30 and 100 % points are one seed; tier 3 (seeds 1 and 2 at 30 and 100 %, 16 runs, about 22 GPU-hours) would complete the picture but is not needed for the conclusions above.
- The 10 % pretrained seed-0 point is a double failure: its SBI/B classifier puts a ratio of 1.4e6 on one toy event and its reference calibration sits 0.65 % low (tier 1, finding 5), on top of the S/B tail. Recalibrating on the validation background removes the SBI/B part, after which the point behaves like the other pretrained 10 % seeds: it spikes near mu = 1 for mu = 0.4 (+0.53, width 0.14) and stays mostly collapsed at mu = 2.5 (41 of 48 open).
- The notebook's automatic decision line ("pretraining helps at 100 %, mu = 0.4") fires on two single-seed points that differ by less than half the statistical error and should not be quoted.
